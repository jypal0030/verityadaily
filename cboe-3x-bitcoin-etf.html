Cboe Files for First US 3x Bitcoin and Ether ETFs: What It Means

The US crypto market just took a step toward its most aggressive product yet: a 3x leveraged Bitcoin ETF.

On August 10, 2026, Cboe BZX Exchange filed for SEC approval to list a group of leveraged commodity ETFs — including the first US 3x daily long Bitcoin and Ether products — under filing SR-CboeBZX-2026-065. The SEC published the filing on August 14, 2026, opening the official review clock. The slate also includes a 3x Gold ETF, per Cboe's rule-filing registry.

Important: a filing is not an approval. But the fact that the SEC has formally published it means the regulatory process is now underway.

What a 3x leveraged ETF actually is

A 3x daily leveraged ETF aims to deliver three times the daily percentage move of the underlying asset — on a single day.

  • Bitcoin rises 5% in a day → the 3x ETF rises roughly 15%.
  • Bitcoin falls 5% in a day → the 3x ETF falls roughly 15%.

The word "daily" is the critical part. These products reset their leverage every single trading day. They do not deliver 3x over a week, a month, or a year — only over one day.

The volatility decay problem, explained with math

This daily reset creates something called volatility decay — and it's the single biggest thing retail investors misunderstand about leveraged ETFs.

Here's a reproducible two-day example:

DayBitcoin moveBTC result3x ETF move3x ETF result
Day 1+10%1.10+30%1.30
Day 2−10%0.99−30%0.91

After two days, Bitcoin is down 1% (1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99). You might expect the 3x ETF to be down 3% — but it's actually down 9% (1.30 × 0.70 = 0.91).

That extra 6% loss is volatility decay. In a choppy, sideways market — exactly what Bitcoin has been through in 2026 — leveraged products bleed value even when the underlying asset goes nowhere. The more volatile the asset, the faster the bleed.

Why this filing matters right now

Context is everything here. Bitcoin is trading near its 2026 low — around $63,000 after a brutal selloff that saw the price fall roughly $40,000 in six weeks from its highs. It's down about 25% year-to-date, and the Fed's decision to hold rates at 3.50–3.75% with no near-term cuts has kept pressure on risk assets.

Launching 3x leveraged products into a bear market is a double-edged sword:

  • For sophisticated traders: leveraged exposure without futures accounts or margin — a new tool.
  • For retail investors: the combination of volatility decay and a declining trend is a wealth-destruction machine. A 3x ETF in a falling market falls roughly three times faster than the asset on a daily basis — and the decay compounds against you over time.

Timeline: what happens next

  • The SEC now has an initial 45-day window to review the filing, with the option to extend the timeline to 90 days.
  • The agency can approve, deny, or extend the review period — and it can also take the full window simply to ask questions.
  • If approved, these would be the first 3x leveraged crypto ETFs in the US, following the 2024 spot Bitcoin ETF approvals and the later leveraged (2x) products.

The bottom line

The filing is a genuine milestone for crypto financial products — but it's also a reminder of how dangerous leverage is for most investors. If these ETFs launch, the daily-reset mechanics mean they are trading instruments, not investments. Anyone considering them needs to understand volatility decay before buying a single share.

Who is this product for?

Honestly, the answer is narrow:

  • Day traders and institutional desks with disciplined risk management may find daily-reset leveraged ETFs useful as a tool.
  • Long-term investors should almost certainly avoid them. The math above is not an edge case — it's how the product works by design.
  • Anyone new to crypto should treat 3x products as a reason to learn about volatility decay, not a shortcut to bigger gains.

A useful comparison: a 3x ETF is closer to a short-dated futures position than to buying Bitcoin. If you wouldn't trade futures, you probably shouldn't trade 3x ETFs either.

What to watch next

Three things will determine how this story develops:

  1. The SEC's decision timeline — a 45-day review window that can extend to 90 days. Any approval would be a first.
  2. Market conditions at launch — if these ETFs debut while Bitcoin is near its 2026 low, early performance will be brutal to watch, and that will shape the product's reputation.
  3. Competitor moves — other exchanges and issuers are likely to file similar products if Cboe's slate gets traction, accelerating the leveraged-crypto arms race.

Either way, the filing itself is now part of the record — and it tells you where the US crypto product market is heading.
