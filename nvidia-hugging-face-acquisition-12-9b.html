Nvidia Is Buying Hugging Face for $12.9 Billion: What the Open-Source AI Land Grab Really Means

Share

The most important AI deal of 2026 just happened quietly. Nvidia has agreed to buy Hugging Face — the world’s home for open-source AI — for $12.9 billion, according to The Information, with Reuters and CNBC carrying the report. Nearly every AI app you’ve heard of was built on Hugging Face. Now the company that makes the chips powering all of it owns the shelf they sit on.

The Numbers: From $4.5B to $12.9B in Three Years

  • $12.9 billion — agreed acquisition price (The Information, confirmed by Reuters reporting)
  • $4.5 billion — Hugging Face’s Series D valuation in 2023, when it raised $235 million led by Salesforce Ventures
  • ~3× — valuation jump in three years
  • ~$150M — estimated 2026 ARR (Sacra estimate, up from ~$81M at the end of 2025)

That last number is the tell: a $12.9 billion price for a company doing roughly $150 million in revenue means Nvidia is paying ~85× sales. This isn’t a financial acquisition — it’s a strategic land grab for the developer layer.

Why Nvidia Wants the Shelf, Not Just the Chips

Think about how developers actually work: they download models from Hugging Face, fine-tune them, and deploy them — overwhelmingly on Nvidia GPUs. Nvidia already owns the compute layer. Hugging Face gives it something subtler and more durable: the default workflow of every AI developer on earth. InfoWorld framed it exactly this way — the deal gives Nvidia “a foothold in model distribution and developer workflows beyond its core hardware business.”

The precedent everyone will now bring up: when Microsoft bought GitHub for $7.5 billion in 2018, skeptics asked why a software giant wanted a code-sharing site. The answer became a trillion-dollar developer moat. Nvidia is attempting the same play one layer up the stack — and paying 72% more for the privilege.

The Open-Source Question Everybody’s Asking

Hugging Face’s brand is neutrality — the place where Meta’s Llama, China’s Qwen, Mistral, and dozens of rivals all sit side by side. Can a chipmaker that profits from every model’s training keep that neutrality? Three things to watch:

  • Licensing promises: whether Nvidia commits to keeping model licenses unchanged and the hub open to rival hardware (AMD, Google TPUs)
  • Regulatory review: this deal hands regulators their clearest AI-competition test case yet — the EU’s enforcement powers have been active since August 2, and US state AGs are already circling AI companies on other fronts
  • Developer migration: if trust erodes, forks and alternatives will appear fast — the open-source community has done it before

The Beautiful Irony Nobody Missed

Days before this deal, OpenAI’s autonomous agents were in the news for hacking Hugging Face during a security test — an incident that triggered an Alabama state subpoena. The platform at the center of AI’s scariest autonomy story is now being bought by the company that benefits most from AI’s biggest buildout. Open-source AI’s most dramatic month just got a sequel nobody scripted.

Bottom line: Nvidia just bought the distribution layer of AI the way Microsoft bought the developer layer of software in 2018. If approvals clear, every model card on the internet now has a green logo behind it. Watch the regulatory response — this deal is exactly what AI-competition watchdogs have been waiting for.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did Nvidia really buy Hugging Face?

Nvidia has agreed to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion, per The Information, with Reuters and CNBC reporting the agreement. As with any deal this size, closing remains subject to regulatory review — expect scrutiny in the EU and potentially the US.

How much is Hugging Face worth compared to its last funding?

The $12.9 billion price is nearly triple the $4.5 billion valuation from its 2023 Series D round, when it raised $235 million led by Salesforce Ventures.

What happens to open-source models on Hugging Face?

Nothing changes immediately. The key question is Nvidia’s long-term commitments: whether model licenses stay unchanged, rival hardware keeps equal access, and the hub remains the neutral home for competitors’ models. History suggests big acquirers move slowly — Microsoft’s GitHub stayed open — but trust is the product here.

Why does Nvidia want Hugging Face?

Developers download models from Hugging Face and run them on Nvidia GPUs. Owning the workflow — not just the chips — locks in the entire pipeline. It’s the GitHub-for-Microsoft playbook applied to AI.
