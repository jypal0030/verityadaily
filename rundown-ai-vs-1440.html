The Rundown AI vs 1440: Which Daily Newsletter Deserves Your Inbox in 2026?

Your inbox is about to get a new tenant. Two newsletters dominate the "stay informed without drowning" category in 2026: The Rundown AI, the AI-first daily brief from Rowan Cheung, and 1440, the human-edited digest that promises the day's news without the spin. They solve the same problem — too much news, too little time — with opposite philosophies. One leans on AI-assisted synthesis and an AI-native beat; the other on human curators and radical neutrality. We compared them across content, sourcing, trust, speed, pricing, and fit so you can decide in the next five minutes which one deserves your inbox — or whether the honest answer is both.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • The Rundown AI is an AI-specialist daily newsletter founded by Rowan Cheung in 2022; the core daily brief is free and ad-supported, with 2M+ subscribers
  • 1440 is a general-interest daily digest founded in 2017 by Tim Huelskamp, grown from 78 subscribers to more than 4 million (as of October 2024), also free
  • Core difference: AI-assisted curation with an AI/tech focus vs human-edited, strictly neutral coverage of politics, business, science, and culture
  • Both are free to subscribe — the real choice is about coverage focus and editorial philosophy, not price
  • They complement each other well: many readers use The Rundown AI for AI developments and 1440 as a neutral daily compass

The Quick Answer

Choose The Rundown AI if AI is your professional edge — you want the latest models, tools, and industry moves distilled into a few skimmable minutes each day. Choose 1440 if you want one calm, neutral briefing that covers the whole day — politics, world news, business, science, culture — with minimal interpretation and clear source links. Neither is universally "better"; they optimize for different readers. The detailed comparison below shows exactly where each one wins.

What Is The Rundown AI?

The Rundown AI is a daily newsletter focused on artificial intelligence, founded by Rowan Cheung in 2022. It grew out of Cheung's AI threads on social media into one of the largest AI newsletters in the world, with a subscriber base that passed 2 million readers. The format is deliberately compact: the day's most important AI developments summarized in bullets you can read in roughly five minutes, with links to the original reporting.

The daily newsletter is free and ad-supported — there is no paywall on the core brief. The company's paid product is The Rundown University, an education arm with courses and guides on using AI tools; subscribers to the free letter will see it promoted, but the daily news itself does not require payment. Audience-wise, it skews toward professionals, founders, and knowledge workers who need to track AI for their work — a demographic that advertisers pay a premium to reach, which is how the free model stays sustainable.

What Is 1440?

1440 is a daily news digest named after the year the printing press was invented — a nod to its mission of broad, unbiased information distribution. Founded in 2017 by Tim Huelskamp (with co-founder Matt Turlinski), it famously began as an email sent to 78 friends and family. By October 2024 the company announced it had passed 4 million subscribers, and Huelskamp has publicly stated the business earns over $20 million per year — remarkable for a product that costs its readers nothing.

The digest lands each weekday morning with roughly a five-minute read covering the day's top stories across politics, world news, business, technology, science, and culture. Every item is written by human editors with links to the original sources, and the editorial line is explicitly neutral: context without commentary. There is no paywall and no premium content tier — the business runs on sponsorships and advertising placed around the digest.

Content Mix and Depth

The two letters barely overlap in coverage. The Rundown AI goes deep on a single beat: model releases, AI tools, startups, funding rounds, regulation, and the research landscape. If a story does not touch AI, it generally does not make the brief. That focus is a feature for its audience — but it means Rundown readers still need another source for everything else happening in the world.

1440 is the opposite bet: breadth over depth. A typical issue moves from the day's top political story to markets, a science item, and a culture piece, each in one or two tight paragraphs. Depth is intentionally limited — you get the "what" and the "why it matters," not a 2,000-word analysis. For readers who want one newsletter to replace a doom-scrolling habit, that restraint is precisely the appeal.

Trust, Sourcing, and AI's Role

The irony of the matchup: the AI-branded newsletter and the human-curated one both rely on human editing — but to different degrees. The Rundown AI uses AI to scan, synthesize, and draft its summaries at scale, with editors reviewing before send. In a beat as fast-moving as AI, that speed advantage is real: developments get covered the day they happen. The trade-off is the known weakness of AI-assisted summarization — occasional terseness or lost nuance — which editorial oversight has to catch.

1440 keeps AI on the sidelines and builds its trust case on process: human editors, primary-source linking, conservative handling of unverified claims, and a visible corrections policy. Coverage is slower to jump on breaking items by design. For readers who rank accuracy and neutrality above immediacy, that process-first approach is the stronger trust signal — and it is auditable, because every summary links to the reporting it is based on.

One practical evaluation tip that applies to both: check any newsletter's sourcing habits for a week. Every claim should trace to an original report, and evolving stories should be marked as such. The two newsletters here both pass that test; most social feeds do not.

Pricing: Both Start Free

Here is where a lot of outdated comparison posts get it wrong. Both core newsletters are free. The Rundown AI's daily brief is free and ad-funded — its paid product, The Rundown University, sells AI courses and guides, not the newsletter itself. 1440's daily digest is likewise free, funded by sponsorships; there is no subscriber-only tier locking the basic product. In practice, the decision costs nothing to test: subscribe to both, read each for a week, and unsubscribe from the one that does not earn its slot in your morning.

Which One Should You Choose?

Choose The Rundown AI if: you work in or around tech, AI literacy is part of your job, you want tool-level practicality rather than abstract trend pieces, and you are comfortable with AI-assisted summaries backed by human review. It is the strongest single daily brief for keeping up with AI specifically.

Choose 1440 if: you want the whole day in one neutral sweep, you prefer human-curated everything, you value calm framing over hot takes, and you would rather have consistency than personalization. It is the closest thing to a daily newspaper front page that respects your time.

Or do what a large share of their readers do: run both. 1440 covers the world; The Rundown AI covers the technology reshaping it. Together they take about ten minutes and replace an hour of unfocused scrolling.

Alternatives Worth a Look

If neither fits, the 2026 field is deep. Morning Brew covers business with a conversational tone; Axios AM delivers politics and policy in its signature smart-brevity format; Stratechery offers deeper tech-strategy analysis at a paid tier; and The New York Times Morning Briefing provides newsroom-backed depth for those who want a legacy anchor. Single-beat readers can add Politico Playbook for politics or Bloomberg's briefings for markets — but for most people, the Rundown-plus-1440 combination already covers the highest-value ground.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is The Rundown AI free?

Yes. The Rundown AI's daily newsletter is free and ad-supported, with no paywall on the core brief. The company's paid offering is The Rundown University, an education platform for AI courses — the daily news letter itself does not require payment.

Is the 1440 newsletter free?

Yes. 1440's daily digest is free to subscribe and has no premium content tier. The business is funded through sponsorships and advertising, and it has been highly successful on that model — co-founder Tim Huelskamp has publicly stated the company earns over $20 million per year.

What is the main difference between The Rundown AI and 1440?

Focus and method. The Rundown AI is a specialist daily brief on artificial intelligence, produced with AI-assisted synthesis and human editorial review. 1440 is a general-interest digest covering politics, business, science, and culture, curated entirely by human editors with a strictly neutral framing.

Which newsletter has more subscribers?

1440. It announced passing 4 million subscribers in October 2024, up from just 78 when it launched in 2017. The Rundown AI passed 2 million subscribers as of 2026 — still among the largest AI-focused newsletters in the world.

Can you subscribe to both newsletters?

Yes, and many readers do — the pairing works because the coverage barely overlaps. A common setup is 1440 as the neutral morning compass for general news and The Rundown AI for fast, focused AI developments. Both are free, so trying the combination costs nothing.

Final Verdict

This comparison has no loser. The Rundown AI is the best daily brief for AI news — fast, focused, and practical, with a free core product that needs no credit card. 1440 is the best daily brief for everything else — neutral, human-edited, and consistent enough to become a habit. Your choice is really a question about your morning: do you want one calm overview of the world, or the sharpest signal on the technology changing it? The only wrong answer is subscribing to neither and letting an algorithmic feed decide what you know.
