S&P 500 Hits Record 7,799: Rally Explained

The S&P 500 closed at an all-time high of 7,798.99 on August 13, 2026, extending a rally that has now erased every doubt from the spring's AI-caution phase. Cooling inflation, steady rate expectations, and another strong week of AI-linked earnings pushed the benchmark to its latest record — its third straight winning week.

Here's what's actually driving the move, how broad it really is, and the three risks that could end it before Labor Day.

The record, by the numbers

The August 13 session was the headline moment. The S&P 500 rose 0.65% to close at 7,798.99, a fresh closing record. The Nasdaq Composite gained 0.81% to 26,803.03, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.13% to finish at 53,839.99.

The rally capped a week in which the index climbed for a third consecutive week. On August 14 it slipped about 0.2% to 7,785.76, but that pullback was shallow — more a pause than a reversal, with the index still within striking distance of its record.

The move follows a pattern set in early August: on August 4, the S&P 500 surged 1.79% to 7,737 in its strongest single-day gain in months, powered by AI-chip earnings and easing Middle East tensions. The August 13 close took the benchmark to its highest level ever — up roughly 14% for 2026 and more than 20% over the past year, according to S&P Dow Jones Indices.

What actually drove it: inflation, not hype

The most important input was the July CPI report, released August 12. Headline inflation cooled to 3.4% year over year from 3.5%, while core inflation eased to 2.5% — both in line with economist forecasts. For markets, the read was simple: the Federal Reserve is not being forced into another hike, and a September cut remains on the table without being locked in.

That matters because the spring's AI selloff was driven by exactly the opposite fear — that strong AI investment would keep inflation sticky and force the Fed to stay restrictive.

The second input was earnings. Nvidia, Intel, Broadcom, and Micron all delivered or guided to results that beat expectations through July and early August, and the AI-infrastructure trade — data centers, memory, power — kept delivering. Tech led the advance on August 13, with Meta among the gainers.

The third input was a retreat in oil. Crude eased through the week as Middle East tensions cooled, taking pressure off the inflation read and giving cyclical stocks room to run.

How broad is the rally?

The honest answer: broader than it was in June, but still top-heavy.

Small caps have participated in fits and starts. The equal-weight S&P 500 is closer to its record than it was a month ago, but the mega-cap AI names still supply most of the index's year-to-date gains. That concentration is both the engine and the vulnerability.

If Nvidia's August 26 earnings disappoint, or if Jackson Hole (August 27-29) signals the Fed is in no hurry to cut, the same handful of stocks that carried the index up could carry it back down.

Three risks before Labor Day

  1. Nvidia earnings (August 26). The stock is the single largest index weight and the market's de facto AI proxy. Guidance — not the quarter — is the swing factor. A $91 billion revenue guide for the current quarter is what the Street expects; anything below that will test the rally.
  2. Jackson Hole (August 27-29). Kansas City Fed's symposium this year is themed around payments, but every word on rates will move markets. A hawkish surprise — signaling no cut until 2027 — would be the clearest near-term trigger for a pullback.
  3. Concentration. The S&P 500 is up roughly 14% in 2026, but much of that sits in a handful of AI names. History suggests record highs built on narrow leadership are more fragile than broad-based ones.

What to watch next

The immediate calendar is quiet — no major macro prints between now and Nvidia's earnings. That means sentiment, flows, and headline risk drive the tape.

For investors, the setup is a classic "good news is good news" regime: cooling inflation supports multiples, while AI earnings justify the leaders' valuations. The risk is that the two converge on one data point — if inflation surprises hot again, both the Fed-cut story and the AI-margin story wobble at once.

The index's record is real, and it was earned by earnings, not speculation. But records don't protect against drawdowns. Watching August 26 and the Jackson Hole speech is the difference between riding the rally and being caught by its turn.

Bottom line

The S&P 500's record close is a genuine milestone — cooling inflation plus strong AI earnings is the rare combination that lifts both growth and valuation. The next two weeks will test whether the rally has legs: Nvidia reports August 26, and the Fed speaks August 27-29. Until then, the path of least resistance remains up, with volatility likely to rise into earnings.
