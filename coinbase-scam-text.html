Got a "Coinbase" Text? Verify It in 60 Seconds (2026 Guide)

Share

That "Coinbase" text about a withdrawal you never made, a login from another country, or a verification code you didn't request - it is a scam, and in 2026 it became one of the most aggressive crypto-smishing waves on record. This guide gives you real scam-text examples, the 60-second verification flow, the official Coinbase contacts (including the real SMS short code), and the exact recovery steps if you already clicked.

🔄 Last updated October 18, 2026: adds the October 4 civil-forfeiture filing over bitcoin traced to Coinbase SMS scams, the BBB's October 15 crypto-text warning, and the full 2025-2026 wave timeline.

Quick answer

Real Coinbase codes only arrive after YOU start a login - and Coinbase will never call or text to ask for one. If a text urges you to call a number, click a link, or share a code, it is a scam: do not respond, do not tap anything, and verify your account by opening the official app directly. Fake texts often come from long area-code numbers (like the 971 wave), while real verification SMS comes from short codes such as 37443. If you already shared a code, change your password now, revoke sessions, and report via the app.

The October 2026 Development: Scams Now Leave a Paper Trail

For most of 2025 and 2026, the "Coinbase text" wave produced county-fraud alerts and local-news warnings - annoying, frightening, mostly unprosecuted. That changed on October 4, when civil-forfeiture filings were lodged over bitcoin traced back to Coinbase SMS scams. The message for victims: blockchain traceability means stolen funds can surface in court actions, and the message for scammers: the paper trail now runs both ways.

Days later, the Better Business Bureau issued its October 15 crypto-text warning, folding Coinbase impersonation into the broader text-message and romance-fraud picture. The pattern across 2025-2026 is consistent: county sheriffs (Middlesex NJ, Washoe NV), state AGs, and consumer agencies kept flagging the same mechanics - and the tap-to-panic playbook hasn't changed.

The Scam Texts, Decoded

These are the recurring templates from the 2025-2026 waves - each with the red flags that give it away:

1. The withdrawal-code scam (the Middlesex County alert template): a text claims a withdrawal was just made from your account and includes a "verification code" you didn't request. Red flags: you initiated nothing; the code exists to make the story feel real; the follow-up asks you to "confirm identity" by reading the code back.

2. The unauthorized-login panic text: "A new device signed in from another country. Securing your account requires immediate verification." Red flags: no such push exists outside the app; urgency is synthetic; the link domain is never coinbase.com.

3. The password-reset text: claims your password was just changed and offers a "reset link." Red flags: real resets happen in-app or via email from coinbase.com domains; the text version routes to a credential-harvest page.

4. The support-hello text: "Hello, this is Coinbase support regarding your account." Red flags: Coinbase does not initiate support by SMS; official phishing guidance explicitly says support contact does not work that way.

5. The 971-area-code variant: same scripts, sent from a long number with an Oregon-area code, sometimes voice-calling after. Red flags: real verification SMS comes from short codes like 37443 - not dialable area-code numbers.

6. The email cousin: the same stories arrive as "Coinbase" emails with lookalike domains. Red flags: check the actual sender domain, not the display name; official guidance covers checking email headers.

"Every template shares one signature: it wants you to act on the text's terms - call, click, or read a code back. Real security steps never start from an inbound SMS."- The consistent finding across county alerts and Coinbase's own guidance

Verify in 60 Seconds

When a "Coinbase" text arrives, run this exact sequence:

  1. Do not tap, call, or reply. Every action the text offers serves the scammer. Even "STOP" replies confirm a live number.
  2. Check the sender. Long area-code numbers (the 971 pattern) are fake by definition. Real verification texts come from short codes like 37443 - and even then, only if you triggered a login.
  3. Open the official app directly. Not via any link in the text. The app's security page shows every real login, device confirmation and withdrawal - if nothing is there, the text invented it.

That's the whole verification. A real security event appears in the app; a fake one only exists in the text. If the app shows a real event, proceed to the recovery section below.

"I Clicked the Link" — The Recovery Playbook

If you already tapped a link, entered a code, or read a code to a caller - act in this order, fast:

  1. Change your Coinbase password now (and anywhere you reused it). Speed matters more than elegance.
  2. Revoke active sessions and reset 2-step in the app's security settings - this kills the attacker's live access.
  3. Check for device-confirmation emails you didn't trigger: per official guidance, that message means someone already used your password and a code - treat it as a breach alarm, not spam.
  4. Contact support in-app and say the words "unauthorized access" - it routes to the security team, and early contact can freeze further movement.
  5. Report it: screenshot the text to security@coinbase.com (the exact path county alerts recommend), file at reportfraud.ftc.gov and IC3.gov, and report the number as junk in your messaging app.
  6. Move nothing "for safety." Any instruction to shift funds to another wallet is stage two of the scam - real support never says it.

Honest expectation-setting: crypto transfers are irreversible and full recovery is rare. The October forfeiture filings show funds can be traced in prosecuted cases, but the playbook's real job is stopping stage two - the drain attempt that follows the first contact.

Why These Scams Work

The mechanics are boring; the psychology is not. Every template hijacks the same reflex - account-panic. A message implying "your money is moving right now" short-circuits the verification instinct, because the cost of ignoring a real breach feels higher than the cost of complying with a fake one. Scammers need exactly one moment of compliance: a code read back, a link tapped, a remote-app installed.

The Coinbase-text wave also rides two bigger 2026 currents: the pig-butchering pipeline that state AGs keep warning about, where crypto scams escalate into long-con romance fraud, and the aggressive targeting of older adults, whom consumer agencies rank among the most-hit groups for financial scams. The BBB's October warning explicitly ties text-message scams and romance fraud into one funnel.

"Report fast, move nothing: the victims who fare best are the ones who treat the first click as a security event, not an embarrassment."- The recovery playbook's core rule
"The scam doesn't need to be convincing - it needs to be urgent. Panic is the product; the code is just the receipt."- Why every template, from NJ to Nevada, follows the same script

FAQs

Why did I get a Coinbase verification code I didn't request?

Either someone is using your password with that 2-step code, or a scammer faked the message to panic you. Never share or enter the code. Open the official app directly: if a real login attempt shows there, change your password immediately and revoke sessions.

What phone numbers does Coinbase use to text?

Real verification SMS comes from short codes - most commonly 37443 - and only after you trigger a login. Real Coinbase texts never include a number to call back and never ask you to return a code.

How do I identify a fake Coinbase text?

Run the four checks: sender (area-code numbers are fake; short codes only), link (non-coinbase.com domains are fake), request (any ask for codes, passwords or installs is fake), tone (urgency and deadlines are scam signatures). One failure means fraud.

Can Coinbase get my money back if I was scammed?

Rarely - crypto transfers are irreversible. Contact support in-app immediately, report to the FTC and IC3, and send screenshots to security@coinbase.com. The October 2026 forfeiture filings show traceability exists in prosecuted cases, but prevention is the reliable strategy.

Is Coinbase a legitimate platform?

Yes - Coinbase is a US-listed, regulated exchange. The texts impersonating it exist because the brand is trusted; the platform is real, the impostors are not.

What if I called the number in the scam text?

Hang up, change your Coinbase password and any reused passwords, revoke sessions, and watch for device-confirmation emails you didn't trigger. If you shared a code or installed software, contact support in-app and file FTC/IC3 reports. Move no funds at anyone's instruction.

Why can't I withdraw my money from Coinbase?

Legitimate causes include pending confirmations and new-payment-method holds. But anyone on a call directing you to move funds to another wallet "for safety" is the scam itself - real support never routes withdrawals externally.

What is the 37443 text from Coinbase?

37443 is the SMS short code users most commonly report for genuine Coinbase verification texts. It only contains codes you triggered by logging in - any request to reply, call or share the code means the message is spoofed.
